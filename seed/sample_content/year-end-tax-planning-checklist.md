The final months of the calendar year are the last window to take actions that affect your current
year's tax return — most tax-year deadlines, unlike the filing deadline itself, fall on December 31,
not the following spring.

## Income and Withholding

- Review year-to-date withholding and estimated payments against your projected total tax liability
  to avoid an underpayment penalty or an unpleasant surprise in April.
- If you had a large one-time event this year — a bonus, a business sale, a large capital gain —
  consider whether an additional estimated payment or withholding adjustment is needed before
  year-end.

## Retirement Accounts

- Confirm you are on pace to maximize workplace retirement plan contributions if that is your goal;
  most plans stop accepting contributions with the final paycheck of the year, not on December 31
  itself.
- IRA contributions, by contrast, can typically be made up until the following spring's tax filing
  deadline and still count for the prior tax year — a useful distinction if you are deciding between
  a 401(k) versus IRA contribution and running out of paycheck cycles.
- Consider whether a Roth conversion makes sense this year, particularly in an unusually low-income
  year, since conversions must be completed by December 31 to count for the current tax year.

## Required Minimum Distributions

- If you are subject to RMDs this year, confirm the withdrawal has been taken (or is scheduled) well
  before December 31 — the penalty for a missed RMD is calculated on the shortfall, and processing
  delays near the holidays are common.

## Tax-Loss Harvesting

- Review taxable brokerage accounts for unrealized losses that could offset gains realized earlier
  in the year, keeping the wash-sale rule's 30-day window in mind if you plan to repurchase a similar
  position.
- Confirm any capital gains distributions your mutual funds are expected to make before year-end,
  since those are taxable even if you do not sell any shares yourself.

## Charitable Giving

- If you itemize deductions, confirm charitable contributions are made (and receipted) by December
  31.
- Consider whether "bunching" two years of planned giving into one tax year — potentially through a
  donor-advised fund — would let you clear the standard deduction threshold and itemize in that year.
- If you are of RMD age, consider a qualified charitable distribution directly from an IRA instead of
  writing a check from other assets, since a QCD is excluded from taxable income entirely.

## Flexible Spending Accounts

- Check your FSA balance and your plan's rules — many FSAs still enforce a "use it or lose it"
  deadline or a limited carryover, unlike an HSA.

## A Final Note

Year-end tax moves are most effective when planned with enough lead time to actually execute them —
waiting until the last few days of December leaves little room for account transfers, trade
settlement, or custodian processing times.
